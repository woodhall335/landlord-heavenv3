William Mumford and Leanne Mumford v Rebecca Mumford | Ground 14 | Flat 7 Machin Mews, Standfast Road,
Henbury, Bristol, BS10 7HJ
Parties tenancy and notice
1. The Claimants are the joint landlords of the Property. The Defendant occupies the Property under a written
tenancy agreement made on 1 April 2024. The agreement provided for a 12 month fixed term and for the
tenancy to continue periodically if the Defendant remained in possession. The current rent is GBP 850 per
calendar month. The agreement states that no deposit was taken.
2. On 22 June 2026 the Claimants served a Form 3A notice seeking possession. The notice relied on
Grounds 13 and 14 of Schedule 2 to the Housing Act 1988. The Claimants preserve that notice unchanged.
This claim relies on Ground 14 only. A copy of the notice served is included in the evidence bundle. The
person who effected service must complete and sign Form N215 using the actual method of service.
Ground 14
3. The Claimants rely on the limb of Ground 14 concerning conduct by the tenant, a person residing in the
dwelling-house or a visitor which has caused, or is likely to cause, nuisance or annoyance to persons
residing in, visiting or otherwise lawfully engaging in activity in the locality, and to persons employed in
connection with housing-management functions.
4. The claim is not based merely on the keeping of a cat or on an alleged blanket prohibition against pets. It is
based on the reported persistent odour, fouling and hygiene impact in the common parts, the reported
behaviour towards cleaning staff, the impact on a neighbouring occupier and visitors, and the reported
obstruction of communal electrical remedial work.
Managing agent and contractor records
5. A managing-agent email addressed to Mr and Mrs W Mumford and referring specifically to Machin Mews
Flat 7 records an ongoing issue reported by residents and building staff. It reports that a cat had been
observed defecating in common areas, creating a health risk and an unpleasant environment, and that the
cat had been reported as acting aggressively towards cleaning staff, including scratching and attempting to
bite. The email requested immediate steps to prevent roaming in shared areas, maintain a clean litter box
and address aggressive behaviour.
6. An email supplied to Leanne Mumford concerning an electrician's attempted communal remedial work
refers to a visit on 23 March 2026 and a second visit when the smell was said to be worse. It states that
communal remedial work could not be completed until the area was cleaned.
7. A BNS maintenance portal record dated 23 March 2026 scheduled a further attendance for 10 April 2026.
The record refers to a smell coming from Flat 7, a foul smell around the area, and the need for cleaning
before testing could be completed.
Neighbour complaint
8. The supplied Flat 8 complaint records that its author owned Flat 8 and had a family member living there.
The author reports personally experiencing a strong and unpleasant odour of cat faeces on repeated visits,
seeing cat faeces in the communal hallway, flies in the hallway and near the French doors of Flat 7, and
smell permeating into Flat 8. The complaint describes a resulting effect on the occupier's wellbeing and
contact with his children and states that Environmental Health would be contacted if the issue continued. The
screenshot does not display the author's identity or date and is relied on as hearsay with that limitation made

Evidence status and reasonableness
9. The Claimants have not obtained a signed witness statement from the managing agent, the electrician, the
cleaner or the Flat 8 complainant. They therefore rely on the retained communications and records as
hearsay and serve a notice identifying that evidence. The Claimants do not represent that the makers will
attend the hearing unless they later agree or are directed to do so.
10. The Claimants say that possession is reasonable because the reported conduct affected several
categories of person, concerned sanitation in shared residential space, was reported to have continued after
intervention, affected a neighbouring home and children visiting it, and interfered with communal electrical
remedial work. The Defendant had been warned and asked to take immediate remedial steps.
11. The Claimants ask for possession of the Property, the court fee and such costs as the Court considers
appropriate. No rent arrears or separate money judgment are claimed.
